How to Turn a Long Video into Vertical Shorts Without Losing the Story

A useful short is more than a random excerpt. It needs a clear opening, a complete idea and a frame that makes sense on a phone. This guide explains how to plan that edit, use ClipForge's Shorts workspace, and check the result before posting.

Start with a single reason to watch

Before trimming your recording, decide what each short should deliver. A strong clip might answer one question, reveal a surprising fact, complete a funny exchange or capture an emotional turning point. A clip that begins midway through a sentence often feels confusing even if the moment is dramatic.

Watch the first two seconds with the sound off and then with the sound on. Ask whether someone who has never seen the original video would understand the setup. If not, start a little earlier or add a short opening title. Keep any necessary context and remove repeated introductions.

Choose the right source and format

Prepare a clean source file that plays normally on the device you are editing with. MP4 with a broadly supported video codec is a practical starting point. MOV is a container format, not a guarantee of browser compatibility; unsupported source codecs may need conversion before browser editing. Direct video-file URLs can also be restricted by the source server's cross-origin rules.

For most vertical Shorts and Reels, choose a 9:16 composition. The frame should keep the speaker's eyes and important actions in view. When converting wide footage, simply cropping to the center can hide a second person, captions or a crucial object.

A step-by-step ClipForge workflow

  1. Open Video to Shorts and select a video that you have the right to edit.
  2. Let the workflow analyze the recording where AI processing is available. It can propose moments, but its scores are recommendations, not editorial decisions.
  3. Review each candidate for a complete beginning and ending. Reject cuts that remove the punchline or change the meaning.
  4. Check the 9:16 framing at the start, middle and end. With two speakers, ensure the crop doesn't favor the wrong person during a response.
  5. Review captions and names carefully, particularly for Urdu or mixed-language dialogue.
  6. Export a short test clip first, play it on your phone and confirm the video, sound and total length before producing a large batch.

How long should a Short be?

There is no single length that makes every video perform better. A quick reaction may work in less than 20 seconds, while a meaningful explanation may require more. A useful editing rule is to remove any segment that doesn't clarify the story, reveal information or deliver a payoff. Avoid stretching a naturally short point to hit an arbitrary target.

Dialogue-driven material needs extra care

In drama and interview footage, a pause can communicate tension. Automatic silence removal may make a scene faster but also alter its rhythm. Watch the joins, keep reaction shots when they carry meaning and verify that the correct speaker stays on screen. If the original includes copyrighted music or footage, editing it does not grant permission to publish it.

Quality checks before posting

  • Story: can a first-time viewer understand the clip?
  • Framing: are faces, gestures and text visible throughout?
  • Text: are captions accurate, readable and within safe margins?
  • Sound: does the voice remain clear without clipping or unexpected silence?
  • Export: does the file open in the destination app and show the expected duration?
Browser editing tip: Keep the tab open during an export. Performance and codec support depend on your browser and device. For a large project, retain your original footage and a copy of any saved project file.

Local-First Video Editing: What Stays on Your Device and What Goes to AI

Not every online video tool uploads your whole recording. Some edits happen on your device while AI tasks use remote services. Understanding that difference helps you decide which files are appropriate for each workflow.

What does local-first editing mean?

A browser can decode videos, draw frames on a canvas and record or encode the result on the same computer or phone. In that workflow, selected video bytes can stay on your device. The browser may still fetch libraries, fonts, updates or other resources from the internet. Local-first does not necessarily mean completely offline.

ClipForge includes workflows that operate on source media in the browser and others that request cloud processing for a specific task. Read the details of the tool you choose rather than assuming every feature handles data in the same way.

How automatic captions change the picture

To generate automatic captions, a speech service must analyze the spoken audio. In ClipForge's cloud-based transcription workflows, audio chunks can be sent to Cloudflare Workers AI and the returned text/timing data is used to make editable captions. AI-assisted moment selection may also process transcript text and related timing information remotely.

This is different from manually typing subtitles or editing a video with a browser-only timeline. For a confidential recording, consider whether the cloud-transcription path is appropriate before selecting it.

Browser project storage is not a cloud backup

Local browser storage, including IndexedDB, may preserve media or project state in that browser profile. Clearing browsing data, switching devices or using private browsing can make local projects unavailable. A JSON project manifest usually does not contain the source video bytes, so keeping only that JSON is not enough to reconstruct every project on another computer.

Save copies of your source videos, finished exports and any available subtitle files. Use clear filenames and keep a separate backup for important material.

Permission matters as much as technology

Before editing someone else's video, make sure you have permission or an appropriate legal basis to use it. A tool can change aspect ratio, add effects or remove pauses, but those edits do not automatically grant rights to republish copyrighted footage, songs or images.

For interviews, internal company meetings or personal footage, consider whether participants have agreed to the intended editing and publishing. Some content may be fine for a private project but not for a public social-media account.

A practical privacy checklist

  • Understand whether the specific tool runs locally or uses cloud AI.
  • Avoid putting personal documents, passwords or sensitive medical information into media you plan to process online.
  • Check that you can delete or clear project files from your own device when finished.
  • Review the destination platform's privacy settings before uploading the rendered video.
  • Keep private originals separate from public-ready exports.
